Your hands never leave the desk.

The laptop camera watches you. You make a small gesture. The computer does the thing — no mouse, no keyboard, no talking to it.

What it does

Put a finger over your lips
You're muted and the screen blanks. For when someone walks up to your desk mid-call.
Thumbs up
Your playlist starts — and an LED on the desk pulses to the beat.
Point, and move your hand in the air
The mouse follows your finger. Pinch your thumb and finger together to click. Scroll by dragging two fingers.
Sweep an open palm across the screen
Screen goes black instantly. Sweep again to bring it back.
Sit still too long
It notices you haven't moved and tells you to stand up and stretch.

How it works

1

The camera finds your hands and face — 21 points per hand, 30 times a second.

2

An AI model reads those points and says which gesture you just made.

3

That gesture is wired to a real action — mute, click, blank the screen.

The part that makes it interesting

You can add a new gesture by typing a sentence. Type "hand on chin" and it works — no retraining, no new data. That's what the foundation model buys us, and it's what we'll ask a judge to try live.
